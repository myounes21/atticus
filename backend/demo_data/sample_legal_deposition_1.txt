IN THE CIRCUIT COURT OF THE 11TH JUDICIAL CIRCUIT IN AND FOR MIAMI-DADE COUNTY, FLORIDA

CASE NO. 2022-001234

DEPOSITION OF: JAMES WILSON
WITNESS ADDRESS: 1234 SW 8TH STREET, MIAMI, FL 33130

DATE: FEBRUARY 22, 2023

REPORTER: BARBARA JONES, RPR

EXAMINATION BY: MS. RACHEL LEE, ESQ.
LEE, SMITH & ASSOCIATES
1001 BRICKELL BAY DRIVE, SUITE 1900
MIAMI, FL 33131

MS. LEE: Mr. Wilson, can you please state your full name and address for the record?

MR. WILSON: My name is James Edward Wilson, and my address is 1234 SW 8th Street, Miami, Florida 33130.

MS. LEE: And can you please tell me a little bit about your background, Mr. Wilson? Where were you born and raised?

MR. WILSON: I was born in Miami, Florida, on October 12, 1975. I was raised in the Miami area, attended local schools, and graduated from Miami Coral Park High School in 1993.

MS. LEE: And after high school, what did you do?

MR. WILSON: I attended Miami Dade College, where I earned an Associate's degree in business administration. After completing my degree, I worked for several years in various administrative positions before starting my own business, a small consulting firm, in 2005.

MS. LEE: And can you tell me a little bit about your business, Mr. Wilson? What type of consulting did you do?

MR. WILSON: My business, Wilson Consulting, provided management and marketing consulting services to small and medium-sized businesses in the Miami area. We helped our clients with business planning, marketing strategy, and operational management.

MS. LEE: And how long did you operate your business, Mr. Wilson?

MR. WILSON: I operated Wilson Consulting from 2005 until 2018, when I sold the business to a competitor.

MS. LEE: And what did you do after you sold your business, Mr. Wilson?

MR. WILSON: After selling my business, I took some time off to travel and pursue other interests. In 2020, I started working as a consultant for a non-profit organization, where I currently work.

MS. LEE: And can you tell me a little bit about the non-profit organization you work for, Mr. Wilson? What type of work do you do there?

MR. WILSON: The non-profit organization I work for is called the Miami Community Development Corporation. We provide economic development services to low-income communities in the Miami area. My role is to provide consulting services to small businesses and entrepreneurs in these communities, helping them with business planning, marketing, and access to capital.

MR. JENKINS: (opposing counsel) Objection, your honor. This line of questioning is irrelevant to the case at hand.

MS. LEE: I disagree, your honor. Mr. Wilson's background and experience are relevant to his credibility and understanding of the events in question.

THE COURT: Overruled. Continue with your questioning, Ms. Lee.

MS. LEE: And can you tell me, Mr. Wilson, how you became involved in the events that are the subject of this lawsuit?

MR. WILSON: Yes. In 2019, I was approached by the defendant, John Smith, who was a friend and former business associate. He asked me to help him with a business project, a real estate development deal in the Miami area.

MS. LEE: And can you tell me more about this project, Mr. Wilson? What was your role in it?

MR. WILSON: My role was to provide consulting services to Mr. Smith and his partners, helping them with the business planning and marketing aspects of the project. I also helped them to identify potential investors and secure funding.

MS. LEE: And did you have any concerns about the project, Mr. Wilson? Any reservations about getting involved?

MR. WILSON: At first, I did. I had some concerns about the project's feasibility and the potential risks involved. But Mr. Smith and his partners assured me that they had done their due diligence and that the project was sound.

MS. LEE: And can you tell me, Mr. Wilson, about the events that occurred on January 10, 2020? What did you witness or experience on that day?

MR. WILSON: On January 10, 2020, I attended a meeting at Mr. Smith's office, where we discussed the project's progress and the plans for moving forward. During the meeting, I witnessed an argument between Mr. Smith and one of his partners, Jane Doe.

MS. LEE: And can you describe the argument, Mr. Wilson? What was said?

MR. WILSON: The argument was about the project's funding and the allocation of resources. Ms. Doe was concerned that Mr. Smith was not being transparent about the project's finances and that he was making decisions without consulting her or the other partners.

MS. LEE: And did you see or hear anything else unusual on that day, Mr. Wilson?

MR. WILSON: Yes. After the meeting, I saw Mr. Smith and Ms. Doe in a heated conversation in the parking lot. They were arguing about the project and Mr. Smith's role in it.

MR. JENKINS: (opposing counsel) Objection, your honor. This is hearsay.

MS. LEE: I disagree, your honor. Mr. Wilson is testifying about his personal observations and experiences.

THE COURT: Sustained. The witness may not testify about the conversation he overheard in the parking lot.

MS. LEE: And can you tell me, Mr. Wilson, about the events that occurred in the following weeks and months? Did you notice anything unusual or suspicious?

MR. WILSON: Yes. In the following weeks and months, I noticed that Mr. Smith was becoming increasingly evasive and secretive about the project's progress. He would not return my calls or respond to my emails, and when I did finally speak with him, he would give me vague and unsatisfactory answers about the project's status.

MS. LEE: And did you have any concerns about Mr. Smith's behavior, Mr. Wilson? Did you suspect that anything was amiss?

MR. WILSON: Yes. I had serious concerns about Mr. Smith's behavior and the project's viability. I suspected that he might be hiding something or that the project was in trouble.

MS. LEE: And can you tell me, Mr. Wilson, about the events that led up to the filing of this lawsuit? What did you learn or discover that prompted you to come forward?

MR. WILSON: In June 2020, I received a call from one of Mr. Smith's former employees, who told me that the project was in serious trouble and that Mr. Smith had been misrepresenting the project's progress to investors. I was shocked and outraged by this information, and I immediately began to investigate further.

MS. LEE: And what did you learn during your investigation, Mr. Wilson? What did you discover about Mr. Smith and the project?

MR. WILSON: I learned that Mr. Smith had been making false representations to investors about the project's progress and viability. I also discovered that he had been using investor funds for personal expenses and that the project was deeply in debt.

MR. JENKINS: (opposing counsel) Objection, your honor. This is speculation and not based on personal knowledge.

MS. LEE: I disagree, your honor. Mr. Wilson is testifying about his investigation and the information he uncovered.

THE COURT: Overruled. Continue with your questioning, Ms. Lee.

MS. LEE: And can you tell me, Mr. Wilson, about your decision to come forward and cooperate with the plaintiffs in this lawsuit? What motivated you to take this step?

MR. WILSON: I was motivated by a desire to see justice served and to protect the investors who had been harmed by Mr. Smith's actions. I also wanted to clear my own name and reputation, as I had been associated with Mr. Smith and the project.

MS. LEE: And can you tell me, Mr. Wilson, about your expectations and hopes for the outcome of this lawsuit? What do you hope will be achieved through this legal action?

MR. WILSON: I hope that the lawsuit will hold Mr. Smith and his co-defendants accountable for their actions and that they will be required to compensate the investors for their losses. I also hope that the lawsuit will serve as a deterrent to others who might engage in similar fraudulent activities.

MR. JENKINS: (opposing counsel) Objection, your honor. This is irrelevant and not material to the case.

MS. LEE: I disagree, your honor. Mr. Wilson's expectations and hopes are relevant to his credibility and motivation for testifying.

THE COURT: Sustained. The witness may not testify about his expectations and hopes for the outcome of the lawsuit.

MS. LEE: And can you tell me, Mr. Wilson, if you have any other information or evidence that might be relevant to this case? Anything that you have not already shared with me or the plaintiffs?

MR. WILSON: Yes. I have some documents and emails that might be relevant to the case. I also have some information about other individuals who might have been involved in the project or who might have knowledge about Mr. Smith's activities.

MS. LEE: And can you tell me, Mr. Wilson, if you are willing to cooperate further with the plaintiffs and provide them with any additional information or evidence that you might have?

MR. WILSON: Yes. I am willing to cooperate fully with the plaintiffs and provide them with any information or evidence that I might have.

MR. JENKINS: (opposing counsel) Objection, your honor. This is not a relevant or material question.

MS. LEE: I disagree, your honor. Mr. Wilson's willingness to cooperate is relevant to his credibility and the potential usefulness of his testimony.

THE COURT: Overruled. The witness may answer the question.

MR. WILSON: As I said, I am willing to cooperate fully with the plaintiffs and provide them with any information or evidence that I might have.

CROSS-EXAMINATION BY MR. JENKINS:

MR. JENKINS: Mr. Wilson, can you tell me about your relationship with the plaintiffs in this lawsuit? How do you know them, and what is your connection to them?

MR. WILSON: I do not have a personal relationship with the plaintiffs. I met them through my work on the project, and I have been cooperating with them in their investigation and lawsuit against Mr. Smith.

MR. JENKINS: And can you tell me, Mr. Wilson, about your motivations for testifying in this case? Are you seeking any personal gain or benefit from your testimony?

MR. WILSON: I am not seeking any personal gain or benefit from my testimony. I am motivated by a desire to see justice served and to protect the investors who were harmed by Mr. Smith's actions.

MR. JENKINS: And can you tell me, Mr. Wilson, about your credibility and reliability as a witness? Have you ever been convicted of a crime or been involved in any questionable or unethical activities?

MR. WILSON: I have never been convicted of a crime, and I have always conducted myself in a professional and ethical manner. I am a credible and reliable witness, and I am committed to telling the truth and providing accurate information.

MR. JENKINS: And can you tell me, Mr. Wilson, about your knowledge of the project and its finances? Did you have any involvement in the project's financial management or decision-making?

MR. WILSON: I did not have any direct involvement in the project's financial management or decision-making. However, I did have some knowledge of the project's finances and budget, and I was aware of some of the financial difficulties that the project was experiencing.

MR. JENKINS: And can you tell me, Mr. Wilson, about your interactions with Mr. Smith and the other defendants in this case? Did you have any conversations or meetings with them that might be relevant to this lawsuit?

MR. WILSON: I had several conversations and meetings with Mr. Smith and the other defendants, particularly in the early stages of the project. However, as the project progressed and I became more aware of the problems and issues that were arising, I had less and less contact with them.

MR. JENKINS: And can you tell me, Mr. Wilson, about your understanding of the project's business plan and strategy? Did you have any concerns or reservations about the project's viability or potential for success?

MR. WILSON: I had some concerns and reservations about the project's viability and potential for success, particularly as I became more aware of the financial difficulties that the project was experiencing. However, I was assured by Mr. Smith and the other defendants that the project was sound and that they had a solid plan in place for its success.

MR. JENKINS: And can you tell me, Mr. Wilson, about your knowledge of the project's marketing and sales efforts? Were you involved in any way in the project's marketing or sales activities?

MR. WILSON: I was not directly involved in the project's marketing or sales activities. However, I did have some knowledge of the project's marketing strategy and plans, and I was aware of some of the challenges that the project was facing in terms of attracting investors and generating sales.

MR. JENKINS: And can you tell me, Mr. Wilson, about your understanding of the project's operational management and organization? Did you have any concerns or reservations about the project's management or leadership?

MR. WILSON: I had some concerns and reservations about the project's management and leadership, particularly as I became more aware of the problems and issues that were arising. However, I was assured by Mr. Smith and the other defendants that the project was being properly managed and that they had a solid team in place.

MR. JENKINS: And can you tell me, Mr. Wilson, about your knowledge of the project's budget and financial projections? Did you have any concerns or reservations about the project's financial plans or expectations?

MR. WILSON: I had some concerns and reservations about the project's financial plans and expectations, particularly as I became more aware of the financial difficulties that the project was experiencing. However, I was assured by Mr. Smith and the other defendants that the project's financial plans were sound and that they had a solid understanding of the project's budget and financial projections.

MS. LEE: (redirect examination) Mr. Wilson, can you tell me about your response to Mr. Jenkins' questions about your credibility and reliability as a witness? Do you have any concerns or reservations about your ability to provide accurate and truthful testimony?

MR. WILSON: I do not have any concerns or reservations about my ability to provide accurate and truthful testimony. I am a credible and reliable witness, and I am committed to telling the truth and providing accurate information.

MS. LEE: And can you tell me, Mr. Wilson, about your response to Mr. Jenkins' questions about your knowledge of the project's finances and budget? Do you have any concerns or reservations about your understanding of the project's financial plans and expectations?

MR. WILSON: I do not have any concerns or reservations about my understanding of the project's financial plans and expectations. I had some knowledge of the project's finances and budget, and I was aware of some of the financial difficulties that the project was experiencing.

MS. LEE: And can you tell me, Mr. Wilson, about your response to Mr. Jenkins' questions about your interactions with Mr. Smith and the other defendants? Do you have any concerns or reservations about your ability to provide accurate and truthful testimony about your interactions with them?

MR. WILSON: I do not have any concerns or reservations about my ability to provide accurate and truthful testimony about my interactions with Mr. Smith and the other defendants. I had several conversations and meetings with them, particularly in the early stages of the project, and I am committed to telling the truth and providing accurate information about those interactions.

THE COURT: That's all the time we have for today, Ms. Lee. Do you have any other questions for the witness?

MS. LEE: No, your honor. I have no further questions.
