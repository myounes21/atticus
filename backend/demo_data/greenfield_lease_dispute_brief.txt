GREENFIELD HOLDINGS v. EAST HARBOR PROPERTIES
Matter Type: Commercial lease dispute (office tower, floors 11-14)
Forum: Anticipated filing in Kings County Commercial Division
Status: Pre-suit demand and response exchanged

Lease Snapshot
- Original lease date: 2019-06-15
- Premises: 58,000 rentable square feet at 490 Atlantic Exchange, Brooklyn, NY
- Current base rent: USD 142,000/month plus operating expense true-up
- Remaining term: through 2029-06-30
- Use clause: general office + client conference space

Disputed Clauses
- Section 7.2 (Landlord Maintenance): Landlord must keep building systems, including base building HVAC, in commercially reasonable working order.
- Section 11.4 (Tenant Self-Help): Tenant may perform required work after 10 business days written notice and offset reasonable costs against rent if landlord fails to cure.
- Section 18.1 (Quiet Enjoyment): Tenant entitled to beneficial use of premises without material interference.
- Section 23.6 (Force Majeure): Excludes payment obligations and excludes predictable equipment replacement delays caused by deferred maintenance.

Factual Timeline
- 2024-02-27: Tenant reports repeated HVAC shutdowns during business hours on floors 12 and 13.
- 2024-03-01: Tenant sends first notice of default citing Section 7.2 and attaches temperature logs showing interior temperatures up to 84 F.
- 2024-03-10: Landlord contractor performs temporary compressor reset; service ticket indicates likely failing chiller control board.
- 2024-03-18: Second maintenance visit replaces relay but not control board; contractor note says "temporary stability expected 2-3 weeks".
- 2024-03-29: Tenant sends second notice, states executive meetings moved offsite, and reserves self-help rights.
- 2024-04-05: Landlord responds that repairs are "substantially complete" and cites regional parts backlog.
- 2024-04-12: Tenant commissions independent HVAC vendor; report recommends full control board replacement and balancing.
- 2024-04-16: Tenant issues intent to offset rent for emergency cooling rentals and consultant fees.

Damages Claimed by Tenant (preliminary)
- Portable cooling rentals: USD 48,600
- Employee relocation and temporary conference space: USD 31,400
- Consultant diagnostics and after-hours support: USD 14,250
- Potential consequential business interruption claim reserved but not yet quantified

Landlord Defenses Preview
- Temporary repair restored usable conditions for most of March and therefore met "commercially reasonable" standard.
- Any prolonged outage tied to supply-chain delay covered by force majeure.
- Tenant failed to provide full 10-business-day cure period before initiating self-help vendor contract.

Key Legal Questions
1) Does repeated temporary repair satisfy Section 7.2 where technician notes recommend full replacement?
2) Can force majeure apply when maintenance records show prior recommendations to replace aging components in 2023?
3) Are tenant offsets valid if landlord performed partial cure actions within notice window?
4) Is there sufficient evidence for breach of quiet enjoyment beyond routine building disruptions?
